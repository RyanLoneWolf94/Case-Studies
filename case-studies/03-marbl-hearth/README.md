## 1. The Client & The Brief

The business came to LoneWolf Digital for **digital marketing**, frustrated at being
"ghosts in the digital space" after twenty years of trading. Rather than take the brief
at face value, I pushed back after diagnosing a deeper, structural problem: **you cannot
market a brand that does not exist yet.**

The deficits were foundational, not cosmetic:

- **No brand architecture** — no strategy, no story, no brand DNA.
- **Missing core assets** — no responsive logo suite, no cohesive visual system.
- **Generic positioning** — "Cakes & Bakes" is a common name with no trademark viability
  and no clear domain, making differentiation from local competitors nearly impossible.

**The core challenge:** translate a genuine *wealth of experience* — two decades of craft
— into a distinct, ownable identity that could command premium value *before* a cent was
spent on growth marketing.

---

## 2. Discovery & Strategy

The engagement was re-scoped from "marketing" to a **full rebrand first, marketing
second**. The strategy was built on three pillars that every later decision traces back
to.

### The succession story (the uncopyable asset)
Marbl Hearth is a story of **legacy passing from one generation to the next**: Mavis, the
founder and keeper of twenty years of craft, and her daughter, the next-generation public
face carrying it forward. This generational hand-over is the single most defensible asset
the brand owns — **no competitor can copy it** — and it doubles as the content engine
(founder teaching, daughter learning).

### The name
**Marbl Hearth** was chosen from an archived longlist:
- **Marbl** references the *marble fold* baking technique — the craft at the centre of the
  work. Dropping the final "e" protects trademark and domain availability.
- **Hearth** evokes the oven, warmth, home, and the gathering place.

### Positioning
- **USP:** *The Modern Legacy* — heritage and experience, delivered with a modern, premium
  finish.
- **Heritage cue:** *"Trusted since '06"* (2006 = twenty years of trading to 2026).
- **Audience:** medium-to-high-income earners, teens to ~45, in Harare — a deliberate
  hedge for a market where affordability, not interest, is the main barrier to purchase.
- **Market read:** the two Harare category leaders proved that a real following comes from
  *a reason to follow beyond the product* (tutorials, ecosystem) or a long physical
  footprint. The succession story is Marbl Hearth's version of that reason.

### Tagline
> **"Legacy, in the rise."**
> *(The "rise" references both dough rising and the ascent of the next generation.)*

## 3. Visual Identity

Every craft decision is **concept-driven, not merely decorative** — each element
physically embodies the marble-fold and succession ideas.

### The Swirl-Heart Monogram
Two swirl strokes form a heart silhouette, with a **negative-space `M`** where they meet
— the brand initial is a *product of the two elements combining*, not an overlay. The
two-tone construction illustrates the **marble fold** (two batters folded together) and
the **generational duality** (two strokes, two generations). The asymmetry in the strokes
is intentional, representing rising energy and motion. Superseded concepts (the Oven Arch,
the Hybrid Badge) were archived.

### Colour system
| Role | Colour | Hex |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | Burnt Terracotta | `#D1672C` |
| Secondary | Sage / Olive | `#717B41` |
| Accent | Dusty Rose | `#F7AAA2` |
| Dark | Deep Espresso | `#5E210F` |
| Light | Warm Cream | `#FEECDE` |

*(The terracotta was re-mastered from an earlier `#BB6B2C`; all logo files were
re-exported in `#D1672C`.)*

### Typography (three tiers)
| Tier | Typeface | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Display | **Bilderberg** | Decorative headers, hero display |
| Body | **Avignon Pro** | Brand body copy, weight-responsive |
| Fallback | **Poppins** | Official documents & everyday business use |

**Small-surface rule:** when Bilderberg loses legibility (embroidery, favicon, van livery
at distance), default to Avignon Pro at the appropriate weight.

### Decorative assets
A **radiating swirl flourish / feather-burst** motif (derived from the mark) and a
**polka-dot band** for collateral.

## 5. Process — Client Interaction to Delivery

```
Diagnosis & strategic pivot  →  Rename (Cakes & Bakes → Marbl Hearth)  →  Brand DNA & strategy
   →  Swirl-Heart logo suite  →  Palette, type & patterns  →  18-page Brand Manual
   →  Corporate collateral  →  Domain, email & channel setup  →  Google Business Profile
   →  Multimedia & packaging  →  Brand Kit handover  →  Paid digital launch (1 Sep 2026)
   →  Month-1 report & mid-cycle pivot
```

**How the work was divided:** strategy, naming, positioning, and all written deliverables
were developed with the client in the loop; all final design craft (logo construction,
colour application, collateral, mockups, motion) was executed in-house to production
standard. The founder films product footage to a moodboard; I handle edit, motion
graphics, and publishing.

**Review pattern:** options were presented at decision points and the client made the
final call; everything was signed off before launch. Client-facing reports are kept short
and precise by request.

**House discipline:** every deliverable carries a clean reference code and versioned
filename, with matched sets (e.g. client brief + companion strategy on identical
letterhead). All account credentials transfer to the client at handover and are never
retained by the studio.

## 6. Outcome

Marbl Hearth went from an invisible, generically-named bakery to a **market-ready brand**
with a defensible story, a complete and signed-off visual system, a live digital presence,
and a paid launch that **out-performed its own targets**:

- **325 followers by the end of Month 1** against an original target of **100**.
- **148 WhatsApp message conversations** generated — a warm lead list opened *two months
  ahead of plan* after a mid-cycle pivot from follower-collection to message ads.
- **~$1.25 per 1,000 views**, half the conservatively modelled $2.50; ~$0.84 per
  conversation against a modelled $1.00.
- All enquiries answered within 24 hours.

The rebrand engagement closed as **complete (23/23 deliverables)**, with the digital
foundation in place to scale. The launch objective for the quarter was **credibility, not
immediate sales** — and the numbers established exactly that.
