## 1. The Client & The Brief

WYFIX is a construction and chemical products manufacturer founded in Harare in 2021 by a
founder named Stan. The company had a real product line — a 21-SKU catalogue spanning
construction chemicals and a hardware range — but no coherent brand to carry it into a
crowded, price-driven market. No positioning, no defensible visual language, and no
packaging system to make the range read as one family on a shelf.

**The core challenge:** build trust fast in a sector where buyers are wary of unproven
local manufacturers, and do it without disappearing into the sea of lookalike blue
branding that already saturates Zimbabwean construction and hardware products.

The engagement was scoped as a **full five-phase brand system**, not a logo-and-done job:

1. **Brand Strategy & Identity**
2. **Visual Identity & Design**
3. **Corporate & Digital Kit**
4. **IT & Digital Presence**
5. **Packaging & Multimedia**

---

## 2. Discovery & Strategy

The strategic foundation was built on a single north-star word that every later decision
had to earn its way back to:

> ### North star: **Trust**
> ### Tagline: *"Authentic. Efficient. Trusted."*

In a market where contractors gamble on whether a local product will actually perform,
trust isn't a soft value — it's the entire purchase decision. The brand was built to
*signal reliability before a buyer has ever used the product*.

### Audience
- **Primary:** SME contractors — buying for the job, judged on whether it works
- **Secondary:** DIY homeowners

### Competitive landscape
A **9-competitor map** was built across international and local tiers to find the open
ground — informing both the positioning and, critically, the colour decision below.

### Product architecture
The **21-SKU catalogue** was treated as a system from the outset, so the identity and
packaging could scale across the full range and a hardware sub-range without fragmenting.

---

## 3. Visual Identity

Every craft decision was **justified against the strategy** — trust, efficiency, and
standing apart from the category — rather than chosen on aesthetic preference.

### The contrarian colour decision
The single most strategic call in the identity: **blue is the most overused colour in
Zimbabwean branding**, so the brand was built on **teal** instead — close enough to read
as credible and industrial, distinct enough to own.

| Role | Colour | Hex |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | Teal | `#0C3B45` |
| Brand constant | Orange | `#FE5F04` |
| Hardware range | Navy | `#00295D` |
| Warm neutral | Off-white | `#F1EBED` |

### Typography
| Use | Typeface | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Display / headlines | **Barlow Condensed** | Industrial, confident, space-efficient |
| Body | **Barlow** | Clean, legible, commercially licensed (Google Fonts) |

### The logo
- A **WF monogram with an embedded checkmark** — the checkmark lives inside the left
  stroke of the `W`, turning the mark itself into a signal of *verified, trusted, done right*
- A mixed-case italic **"WyFix"** wordmark
- The **italic lean references forward motion and efficiency** — the "Efficient" in the tagline, drawn

### Packaging system
A **unified architecture** designed to make 21 SKUs read as one family:
- A shared **off-white header** across every pack
- A **colour-coded base zone** to distinguish product lines and the hardware range at a glance
- Developed across **~20 mockups**, treated as concept/family designs for sign-off — not
  production files

## 5. Process — Client Interaction to Delivery

```
Client interaction (Stan)  →  Discovery & strategy  →  Competitor mapping
     →  Positioning & tagline  →  Colour & type  →  Logo system
     →  Packaging architecture (~20 mockups)  →  Brand manual + multimedia
     →  Dual dashboards (internal + client-facing)  →  Handover portal
```

**Division of labour:** strategy, research, competitor mapping, positioning, and written
deliverables were developed across the full lifecycle; all final design craft — logo
construction, colour application, and packaging mockups — was executed in-house to a
professional production standard.

**Review discipline — two decisions worth naming:**

1. **Manual build over auto-generation.** Canva's auto-generated packaging slides were
   rejected because they substituted placeholder imagery for the professionally designed
   product photos. The packaging presentation decks were rebuilt **manually** to keep the
   work client-grade.

2. **Internal vs. client-facing separation.** Two dashboard versions were produced. The
   client-facing version was **fully sanitised** — no internal workflow language, no open
   items visible to the client, and no interactive elements exposing LoneWolf's internal
   process. Internal agency documentation was kept strictly separate from anything Stan sees.

**Open QA items** flagged on the 19-page manual and tracked to resolution: a colour-palette
page rendering invented bar charts instead of true swatches, a stray logo fragment on
page 5, and a purple artifact on a bottle mockup on page 16.

## 6. Outcome

WYFIX moved from a founder with a product range and no brand to a **trust-led brand system**
with a defensible strategy, a deliberately contrarian teal identity that stands apart from
its entire category, a checkmark-bearing logo that encodes the north-star value into the
mark itself, and a unified packaging architecture ready to scale across all 21 SKUs. Phases
1, 2 and 5 are delivered; the Corporate/Digital Kit and IT/Digital Presence phases remain
in progress.
